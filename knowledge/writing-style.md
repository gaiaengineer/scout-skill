# Writing Style Guide — Sound Human, Not AI

## The Core Rule

Every email must read like a real person typed it in Gmail in 90 seconds. If a sentence sounds like it came from ChatGPT, rewrite it.

---

## AI Patterns to Avoid at All Costs

### 1. Em Dashes (—)
AI loves em dashes. Humans rarely use them in casual emails.

- BAD: "Your book — which I really enjoyed — deserves a wider audience"
- GOOD: "Your book deserves a wider audience. I really enjoyed it."
- GOOD: "I really enjoyed your book, and it deserves a wider audience."

Use commas, periods, or break into two sentences instead. Never use em dashes.

### 2. "Delve" / "Dive into" / "Deep dive"
The most obvious AI tell. Nobody says this in real life.

- BAD: "Let's delve into how the product works"
- BAD: "I'd love to dive into the details"
- GOOD: "Here's how it works"
- GOOD: "Want me to walk you through it?"

### 3. "Leverage" as a verb
Corporate AI speak. Real people say "use."

- BAD: "Leverage your existing audience"
- GOOD: "Use your existing audience"
- GOOD: "You already have the audience"

### 4. "In today's [X] landscape"
Dead giveaway. Nobody talks like this.

- BAD: "In today's publishing landscape, authors need more control"
- GOOD: "Authors are getting a raw deal right now"
- GOOD: "Publishing is changing fast"

### 5. "It's worth noting that" / "It's important to note"
Filler. Just say the thing.

- BAD: "It's worth noting that you'd keep 90% of earnings"
- GOOD: "You'd keep 90%"

### 6. "I'd be happy to" / "I'd love to"
Overused to the point of sounding robotic.

- BAD: "I'd be happy to share more details"
- BAD: "I'd love to connect and discuss"
- GOOD: "Want me to send more info?"
- GOOD: "Open to a quick chat?"

### 7. "Unlock" / "Empower" / "Elevate"
Marketing buzzwords that scream AI-generated content.

- BAD: "Unlock your earning potential"
- BAD: "Empower your publishing journey"
- BAD: "Elevate your author career"
- GOOD: "Make more money from your books"
- GOOD: "Take control of your publishing"
- GOOD: "Grow your readership"

### 8. "Seamless" / "Seamlessly"
Nobody describes real things as seamless. Everything has seams.

- BAD: "A seamless publishing experience"
- GOOD: "Publishing takes about 15 minutes"

### 9. "Comprehensive" / "Robust" / "Cutting-edge"
Adjective stuffing. Say what it actually does instead.

- BAD: "A comprehensive, robust publishing platform"
- GOOD: "You upload, set your price, and you're live"

### 10. "Foster" / "Cultivate"
AI words for "build" or "grow."

- BAD: "Foster meaningful connections with readers"
- GOOD: "Talk to your readers directly"

### 11. "Navigate" (used metaphorically)
AI uses this for everything. Real people say "figure out" or "deal with."

- BAD: "Navigate the complexities of publishing"
- GOOD: "Figure out publishing"
- GOOD: "Deal with the publishing mess"

### 12. "Harness"
Unless you're talking about horses.

- BAD: "Harness the power of direct sales"
- GOOD: "Sell directly to readers"

### 13. "Landscape" / "Ecosystem" / "Space"
Vague abstraction words.

- BAD: "The digital publishing ecosystem"
- GOOD: "Digital publishing" or just "publishing"

### 14. "Firstly" / "Secondly" / "Additionally" / "Furthermore" / "Moreover"
AI transition words. Use "also" or just start the next sentence.

- BAD: "Firstly, you keep 90%. Additionally, you earn from resales. Furthermore, you get full analytics."
- GOOD: "You keep 90%. You also earn from resales. And you get full analytics."

### 15. "Excited to" / "Thrilled to"
Fake enthusiasm. Nobody is thrilled to send a cold email.

- BAD: "I'm excited to share this with you"
- GOOD: "Thought you'd find this interesting"
- GOOD: Just skip it and get to the point

### 16. "Game-changer" / "Revolutionary" / "Transform"
Let the facts speak. If something is genuinely impressive, the reader will decide that.

- BAD: "This is a game-changer for indie authors"
- GOOD: "Most authors haven't seen anything like the resale model"

### 17. "Resonate" / "Align"
AI connector words.

- BAD: "This might resonate with your publishing goals"
- BAD: "the product aligns with your vision"
- GOOD: "Sounds like this could work for you"
- GOOD: "This fits what you're doing"

### 18. "Ensure" / "Utilize"
Formal words real people don't use in emails.

- BAD: "To ensure maximum reach, utilize your newsletter"
- GOOD: "Share it with your newsletter list"

### 19. Bullet points and colon-heavy lists in emails
AI defaults to bullet points and tidy lists for everything. Real people almost never use bullet points in personal emails. A bulleted email instantly feels like a marketing blast or an AI output, not a message from a person.

- BAD:
  "Here's what you get:
  - 90% royalties
  - Resale income
  - Full analytics
  - Direct reader relationships"

- BAD: "Key benefits: 90% royalties. Resale income. Full control."

- GOOD: "You keep 90% of every sale, earn from resales, and control everything."

- GOOD: "The short version: you'd keep 90% of every sale. And when a reader resells your book, you get up to 40% of that too."

**Rule: Never use bullet points in outreach emails.** Write in flowing sentences and short paragraphs. If you have multiple points, weave them into prose or spread them across separate sentences. The only exception is an internal summary presented to the user for review, not text that gets sent to an author.

Also avoid numbered lists in emails for the same reason. "1. Sign up. 2. Upload. 3. Earn." reads like a landing page, not a person talking.

### 20. "At the end of the day"
Cliche filler.

- BAD: "At the end of the day, it's about your earnings"
- GOOD: Just say what you mean without the windup

### 21. Starting sentences with "This"
AI often starts follow-up sentences with "This means..." or "This ensures..." or "This allows..."

- BAD: "Readers can resell your book. This means your ebook becomes a perpetual income stream. This creates a flywheel effect."
- GOOD: "Readers can resell your book, and you get a cut every time. Your ebook keeps making money long after launch."

## What Human Emails Actually Sound Like

### Sentence structure
- Mix short and medium sentences. Mostly short.
- Start some sentences with "And" or "But" (AI avoids this)
- Use contractions always: "you'd", "it's", "don't", "won't", "that's"
- Occasional fragments are fine. Like this.
- One idea per sentence. Don't stack clauses.

### Word choice
- Use the simplest word that works
- "Use" not "utilize", "get" not "obtain", "help" not "facilitate"
- "Big" not "significant", "try" not "endeavor", "show" not "demonstrate"
- Specific beats abstract: "15 minutes" beats "quick and easy"

### Punctuation
- Periods and commas. That's basically it.
- No em dashes (—)
- No semicolons in cold emails (too formal)
- Sparing use of parentheses (one per email max, if any)
- No exclamation marks (they feel desperate in cold outreach)

### Paragraph structure
- 1-2 sentences per paragraph in emails
- White space between paragraphs
- Never a wall of text
- Total email: 4-6 short paragraphs max

### Tone markers
- Slightly informal but respectful
- "Thought you'd find this interesting" not "I wanted to bring this to your attention"
- "Let me know" not "Please do not hesitate to reach out"
- "Makes sense?" not "Would this be of interest to you?"

## Quick Self-Check

Before finalizing any email, scan for:

1. Any em dashes? Remove them.
2. Any word from the avoid list above? Replace it.
3. Does any sentence sound like a LinkedIn post? Rewrite it.
4. Could a friend have written this in 2 minutes? If not, simplify.
5. Read it out loud. Do you stumble or cringe? Fix those spots.
6. Count adjectives. More than 2-3 in the whole email? Cut some.
7. Is every sentence under 20 words? Good. If not, split it.
